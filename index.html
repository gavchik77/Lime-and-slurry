What does “weighted average total N” mean?

Different parts of the grazing platform receive different total N rates by 1 April. Because those parts are different sizes, you should not simply add the four N rates and divide by four.

Instead, multiply each N rate by the number of hectares receiving it, add all of the kg N applied, then divide by the whole grazing-platform area.

Weighted average N = [(12.8 × 70) + (4.8 × 75) + (4.8 × 83) + (9.6 × 79)] ÷ 32 = 75.4 kg N/ha

This tells you the average N application per hectare across the entire 32 ha grazing platform, while still allowing each part of the platform to receive a different N rate.

Calculate the areas first, then the weighted N total.

Lesson 4 · Slurry inventory

How much of the 1,000 m³ slurry store is used in early spring?

For volume conversion use 1 ha = 2.471 acres and 1 imperial gallon = 4.546 L. The source gives 2,000 and 2,500 gal/ac application rates.

m³ slurry = area (ha) × 2.471 × gallons/acre × 4.546 ÷ 1000
Use the actual farm area and application rates.

Lesson 5 · First-cut silage

Prioritise slurry to the 8 ha silage block

The silage block is P Index 3 and K Index 3, with a target yield of 5 t DM/ha. Use 33 m³/ha (about 3,000 gal/ac) cattle slurry by LESS.

On Index 3 soils, Teagasc guidance treats 33 m³/ha good-quality cattle slurry as approximately sufficient for 1st-cut silage P and K; then balance N.

Lesson 6 · Timing first-cut N

When should the silage N be applied?

The crop takes up about 2.5 kg N/ha/day. Apply N at least 6 weeks before cutting.

Farm plan completed

What the student should conclude

Lime: correct the 8 ha acidic block from the soil test, using 6.25 t/ha (50 t total).

Sequence: where early slurry is needed, slurry first, then lime after about 7 days; protected urea does not require a lime interval.

Spring grazing: use the well-drained 40/15/15/30 slurry/protected-urea programme.

Slurry: use measured inventory strategically, prioritising low-cover spring areas and the Index-3 silage block.

Silage: use about 33 m³ slurry/ha and balance remaining N with protected urea + S; apply N at least six weeks before cutting.

This case is deliberately simplified. Real farm plans must use current soil tests, actual slurry analysis/DM, crop requirements, stocking-rate/legal nutrient allowances, weather/trafficability and current regulations.
